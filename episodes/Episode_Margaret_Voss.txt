# HOUSE M.D.

## "ORIGAMI"

**TEASER**

INT. PRINCETON-PLAINSBORO HOSPITAL — DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE OFFICE — DAY

HOUSE sits at his desk, cane resting against the armrest. He drums his fingers on a patient chart, expression unreadable. CAMERON, CHASE, and FOREMAN stand around the whiteboard, covered in crossed-out differential diagnoses.

CAMERON
We've ruled out lupus, rheumatoid disease, every autoimmune panel we could throw at her. The ANA's clean.

FOREMAN
Which leaves us with... what? Hypermobility syndrome? That doesn't explain the hepatosplenomegaly or the thrombocytopenia.

HOUSE
(not looking up)
Or it explains all of it perfectly, and you're all too busy looking at the forest to notice the trees are made of cardboard.

CHASE
Sir?

HOUSE
A fifty-two-year-old woman whose shoulders dislocate like party tricks. Elevated liver enzymes. Platelets dropping. Spleen the size of a grapefruit. You're thinking inflammation. Infection. Immune system gone haywire.

(finally looks up)

What if the problem isn't what her body's fighting—what if it's how her body's built?

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — CONTINUOUS

MARGARET VOSS (52), lies in bed, her frame thin but oddly flexible. A bruise darkens her forearm. She stares at the ceiling tiles, counting silently, her jaw tight.

The door opens. HOUSE enters, cane first.

HOUSE
Your mother kept you in ballet until age sixteen.

MARGARET
(startled)
How did you—

HOUSE
Unusual commitment for a kid whose spine was basically origami. But here's what I need to know: when someone touches your arm, does your skin bruise if they're not careful?

MARGARET
(hesitant)
Yes. I thought everyone did that.

House's expression doesn't change, but something shifts in his eyes.

HOUSE
Your collagen—the structural glue holding your skin, joints, and connective tissue together—is defective at the molecular level. Not weak. Malformed.

**END TEASER**

---

**ACT ONE**

INT. DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE OFFICE — DAY

House stands at the whiteboard, writing furiously. He circles "COLLAGEN DISORDER" and underlines it twice.

FOREMAN
Ehlers-Danlos?

HOUSE
Classic EDS doesn't explain the systemic involvement. We need tissue architecture—a skin biopsy. And genetic screening. Full panel.

CAMERON
If it's genetic, there's no cure. Just management.

HOUSE
Then we'd better manage before she bleeds out from something that sounds minor.

He turns to face them.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
She's been this way her whole life. Built wrong from the factory. The question is whether her body's finally figured it out.

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — LATER

A NURSE inserts an IV. Margaret watches her own arm, expecting bruising.

MARGARET
(quietly)
How long have I been broken?

HOUSE
(from the doorway)
Since your first cell divided. You just didn't notice until it mattered.

He enters, closing the door behind him.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Tell me about your childhood. Any fractures that seemed minor but caused major damage?

MARGARET
I fell off a horse when I was twelve. Broke three ribs. The doctor said I was lucky. But my parents... they knew something was different. They never said it, but I could tell.

HOUSE
Different how?

MARGARET
I was too flexible. Too fragile. My mother was terrified I'd shatter.

House absorbs this. He leans against the wall, processing.

HOUSE
She wasn't wrong.

**ACT TWO**

INT. DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE OFFICE — DAY

The team gathers around lab results. The skin biopsy shows abnormal collagen architecture.

CHASE
Reduced type VI collagen fibrils. This is—

FOREMAN
A collagen synthesis disorder.

CAMERON
Multi-system involvement. The dislocations, the hepatosplenomegaly, the bleeding issues—they're all the same problem.

HOUSE
(sitting)
The body's instruction manual was written in crayon. Everything downstream from that is just consequences.

He stands, grabbing his cane.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Genetic panel. Now. And while we wait, we start supportive care. Blood work shows she's hemorrhaging. Transfusion.

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — LATER

A bag of blood hangs from an IV pole. Margaret watches it drip, her expression distant.

MARGARET
(to herself)
Swiss cheese. That's what he said. Swiss cheese instead of collagen.

The door opens. HOUSE enters with results.

HOUSE
Your genetic panel came back. COL6A1 mutation. Your type VI collagen is structurally defective—not just weak. Wrong from the blueprint.

MARGARET
Kyphosco
