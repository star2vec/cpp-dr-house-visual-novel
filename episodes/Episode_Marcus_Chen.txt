# HOUSE M.D.

## "THE STORAGE PROBLEM"

**TEASER**

INT. PRINCETON-PLAINSBORO TEACHING HOSPITAL — DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE BULLPEN — DAY

HOUSE sits at his desk, tossing a tennis ball against the wall. CAMERON and FOREMAN enter with a chart.

CAMERON
45-year-old male, Marcus Chen. Admitted with progressive leg weakness over six months. Can barely climb stairs.

HOUSE
(not looking up)
Muscular dystrophy?

FOREMAN
Negative. And no signs of inflammatory disease.

House catches the ball. His eyes narrow.

HOUSE
Blood work?

CAMERON
That's where it gets weird. Creatine kinase through the roof—3,840. Myoglobin elevated. But liver's angry too. ALT, AST both elevated.

HOUSE
(leaning back)
Muscles are dissolving. Not the liver. The liver's just getting splatter damage.

He stands abruptly, wincing slightly.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Get him a nerve conduction study. And a muscle biopsy. We're looking for something that's breaking down from the inside.

---

**ACT ONE**

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — CONTINUOUS

MARCUS CHEN, 45, sits on the edge of the hospital bed. His legs don't swing naturally—they move with visible effort. HOUSE enters, followed by the team.

MARCUS
(weak smile)
Doc. Tell me you know what's wrong.

HOUSE
Not yet. But I know what's right—your nerves work fine. Your brain's sending signals just dandy. The problem's in the receiving end.

MARCUS
My legs.

HOUSE
Your muscles. They're storage units that forgot how to empty out the trash.

House taps the chart against his palm.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
You quit your gym membership three years ago. Why?

MARCUS
(confused)
How did you—

HOUSE
Because you didn't quit. Your legs did. Am I close?

Marcus nods slowly, shame crossing his face.

MARCUS
I couldn't keep up anymore. Thought I was just getting old.

HOUSE
You're not old. You're broken. But not the way you think.

---

**ACT TWO**

INT. HOSPITAL CONFERENCE ROOM — LATER

CAMERON, FOREMAN, and HOUSE huddle around lab results.

FOREMAN
Biopsy came back. Glycogen accumulation in the muscle fibers. Massive amounts.

CAMERON
And the genetic panel flagged something in the secondary metabolic screen. Acid alpha-glucosidase deficiency.

House closes his eyes. A moment of clarity.

HOUSE
Pompe Disease.

FOREMAN
Adult-onset glycogen storage disease type two. It's rare.

HOUSE
Not rare. Just missed. Because people like us keep looking for the dramatic—the autoimmune attack, the neurological breakdown. But sometimes the answer is simpler. Your body makes fuel it can't burn. The warehouse fills up. The muscles drown.

CAMERON
Is there treatment?

HOUSE
Enzyme replacement therapy. Alglucosidase alfa. We give him the enzyme his body forgot to make, and his muscles stop suffocating under their own leftovers.

---

**ACT THREE**

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — NIGHT

MARCUS lies in bed. HOUSE enters alone, moving with his cane. He pulls a chair close.

HOUSE
We know what you have. Pompe Disease. Your muscles can't break down glycogen—the stored form of glucose. It's piling up inside the cells, making them weak, making them fail.

MARCUS
(voice shaking)
Can you fix it?

HOUSE
No. But we can stop it. Enzyme replacement therapy. You'll get infusions regularly. Your muscles will start clearing out the backup. You might get some strength back. You definitely won't get worse as fast.

MARCUS
And without it?

HOUSE
Your heart's a muscle too. Eventually, it runs out of room.

Marcus stares at the ceiling. Not acceptance. Understanding.

MARCUS
(whispered)
I thought I was just weak.

HOUSE
You were. Now we know why. That's not nothing.

---
