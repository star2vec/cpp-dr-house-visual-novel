# HOUSE M.D.

## "PROGEROID"

**TEASER**

INT. PRINCETON-PLAINSBORO TEACHING HOSPITAL - DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE OFFICE - DAY

HOUSE sits at his desk, tossing a tennis ball against the wall. It bounces back. He catches it without looking.

The door opens. CAMERON enters with a chart.

CAMERON
Forty-five-year-old female, Margaret Chen. Admitted with joint hypermobility, shoulder dislocations, and elevated liver enzymes.

HOUSE
(still tossing)
Hypermobility in a forty-five-year-old isn't a party trick—it's a symptom of something eating her connective tissue. What else?

CAMERON
Skin showing signs of premature aging in non-sun-exposed areas. Dermatology's stumped.

House catches the ball. Stops.

HOUSE
Run a blood panel. Full metabolic workup. If her liver's already checked out, we're not looking at infection or simple cirrhosis.

CAMERON
What are we looking at?

HOUSE
(standing, limping toward the door)
Something systemic. Something that's decided her joints and liver are old friends.

He exits, leaving Cameron with the chart.

**ACT ONE**

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM - MARGARET'S BED - DAY

MARGARET CHEN, 45, sits propped up against pillows. Her movements are careful, deliberate. A shoulder brace hangs loosely on her left arm. Her skin, visible on her forearms, has a peculiar papery quality—thin, almost translucent in places.

HOUSE enters with CAMERON and FOREMAN behind him.

HOUSE
(examining her hands)
How long have your fingernails been growing slower than they used to?

MARGARET
(uncertain)
I... I'm not sure. A year? Maybe longer. Why?

HOUSE
Because you've compensated by filing them obsessively. But that's not the real problem.

He sits on the edge of the bed uninvited.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Your skin. The areas that don't see sunlight—your inner arms, your thighs—does it feel papery? Like tissue paper?

Margaret hesitates. Nods slowly.

MARGARET
How did you—

HOUSE
That's not aging, Margaret. That's your body betraying you at the cellular level.

INT. DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE CONFERENCE ROOM - DAY

House stands before a light box, studying lab results. The team sits around the table.

HOUSE
Blood panel shows elevated transaminases. AST 187, ALT 203. Low albumin at 3.1. Mild thrombocytopenia. The liver's already checked out.

CAMERON
Autoimmune?

HOUSE
ANA panel's negative. No lupus, no rheumatoid factor. We're barking up the wrong immunological tree while her liver fibroses and her joints fall apart like she's made of wet tissue paper.

FOREMAN
Metabolic disorder?

HOUSE
Comprehensive metabolic panel confirms hepatic synthetic dysfunction. Elevated transaminases at nearly triple normal. Prolonged prothrombin time. But it doesn't tell us *why* a forty-five-year-old's collagen is turning to ash.

He limps to the window.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Heavy metal screen comes back clean. Lead, mercury, cadmium, arsenic—all normal. So it's not environmental poisoning. Just a patient whose connective tissue is coming apart at the seams for reasons we haven't touched yet.

INT. DIAGNOSTIC MEDICINE CONFERENCE ROOM - LATER

HOUSE sits. A thyroid panel is displayed on the screen behind him.

HOUSE
Thyroid's fine. TSH 2.1, normal free T4, no antibodies. So we're chasing the wrong endocrine horse.

Cameron frowns, frustrated.

CAMERON
We've ruled out everything.

HOUSE
We've ruled out everything obvious. That leaves us with the impossible.

He stands abruptly.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Order a genetic panel. Full connective tissue workup. If her body's systematically destroying itself, the blueprint's got to be flawed.

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM - MARGARET'S BED - NIGHT

IV fluids drip into Margaret's arm. She stares at the ceiling, exhausted.

HOUSE enters quietly.

HOUSE
Giving you water buys us time to think. Doesn't fix what's eating your connective tissue and destroying your liver from the inside out.

MARGARET
(quietly)
What is it, Doctor? What's wrong with me?

HOUSE
That's what we're going to find out.
