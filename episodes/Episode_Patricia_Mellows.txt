# HOUSE M.D. — "STORAGE UNIT"

**TEASER**

INT. PRINCETON-PLAINSBORO TEACHING HOSPITAL — PATIENT ROOM — DAY

PATRICIA MELLOWS (early 30s, formerly athletic, now visibly struggling) sits upright in bed. Her eyes dart involuntarily—quick, jerky movements she can't control. A glass of water sits untouched on the bedside table.

NURSE enters, checks vitals.

**NURSE**
How are you feeling today, Patricia?

**PATRICIA**
(voice slightly slurred)
My legs... they're not doing what I tell them to. And my eyes won't stop moving.

Patricia reaches for the water. Her hand trembles. She lifts the glass. Attempts to drink. Water dribbles down her chin. She coughs violently.

**NURSE**
(concerned)
I'll get the doctor.

---

**ACT ONE**

INT. DIAGNOSTIC CONFERENCE ROOM — CONTINUOUS

HOUSE sits at the head of the table, leg propped on a chair. CAMERON, CHASE, and FOREMAN stand around the whiteboard covered in differential diagnoses, all crossed out.

**HOUSE**
Okay, let's review our greatest hits: not MS, not ALS, not myasthenia gravis. Nerves are normal. Spinal fluid is clean. Brain's angry but we don't know why.

**FOREMAN**
The blood work shows elevated acid phosphatase and chitotriosidase. That's storage markers.

**HOUSE**
Storage. Right. Like her cells are little hoarders who forgot to take out the trash.

**CAMERON**
Family history?
(pause)
Did anyone actually ask about family history?

HOUSE stands abruptly, grabs his cane.

**HOUSE**
Chase, you still think mitochondrial?

**CHASE**
The progressive decline, the eye movements, the dysphagia—it fits.

**HOUSE**
It fits a lot of things. That's the problem.

He limps toward the door.

**HOUSE** (CONT'D)
Get a genetic panel. And someone talk to the family.

---

INT. PATIENT ROOM — LATER

HOUSE enters. PATRICIA's eyes are still moving. She looks terrified.

**HOUSE**
Your brain's been playing storage unit with things it should've thrown away—and now the whole warehouse is backing up.

**PATRICIA**
What do you mean?

**HOUSE**
You're confused—look, never mind that. What I want to know is why your brain seems to be filing things away in the wrong drawer: you walk like your legs forgot how to talk to your cerebellum, your eyes won't track smoothly, and somewhere in that noggin of yours, lipids are piling up like a hoarder's garage because your cells can't move them out.

**PATRICIA**
That doesn't make sense.

**HOUSE**
Yeah, well, neurology rarely does—but here's what does: your liver's enlarged, you're suddenly clumsy, and your eyes are doing that weird vertical gaze thing like they're stuck in an elevator. Something's jamming up the machinery at the cellular level, and I'm betting it's not just bad luck.

**PATRICIA**
My heart?

**HOUSE**
Your heart's fine—forget about it. What matters is that your neurons are drowning in their own waste because something's broken in how your cells are supposed to clean house, and it's spreading to your brain like a backed-up sewer system.

**PATRICIA**
Is it serious?

**HOUSE**
Yeah, it's serious—but here's the thing: you've got Niemann-Pick disease type C, which means your cells can't move cholesterol out of their lysosomes properly. The diagnostic clue we missed until now? That vertical supranuclear gaze palsy—your eyes won't look up or down on command because the neurons controlling that movement are already drowning in lipid buildup. Everything else—the ataxia, the liver enlargement, the cognitive decline—it all traces back to that one cellular traffic jam.

Patricia's eyes continue their involuntary dance. Understanding and fear wash across her face simultaneously.

**PATRICIA**
Can you fix it?

HOUSE pauses. He doesn't answer. He turns and limps out, leaving the question hanging.

---

**WE ARE NOW THE PATIENT**

INT. PATRICIA'S MIND — CONTINUOUS

*PATRICIA MELLOWS thinks:*

*I don't understand why my legs feel so heavy, like they're not listening to what I'm telling them to do anymore. The room smells sharp and clean in a way that makes everything feel too real and frightening all at once. My eyes keep jumping around on their own, and I can't seem to make them focus on anything—it's making me dizzy and more scared. When I tried to drink some water earlier, it went down wrong somehow, and now I'm afraid to even swallow because I don't know what my own body is going to do next.*

---

**END OF EPISODE**
