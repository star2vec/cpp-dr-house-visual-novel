HOUSE M.D. — "Marcus Webb"
============================================================

# HOUSE M.D. — "STIFF"

**TEASER**

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — DAY

MARCUS WEBB (50s, once athletic, now rigid) lies in bed, staring at ceiling tiles. His right foot twitches involuntarily. A NURSE adjusts the IV drip. Marcus's jaw clenches.

MARCUS
(to himself)
One... two... three... four...

His left leg spasms. He grips the bedsheet.

MARCUS (CONT'D)
Come on. Come on.

The door opens. DR. HOUSE (50s, cynical, brilliant) enters with his cane, followed by CHASE, CAMERON, and FOREMAN.

HOUSE
So you're the guy who forgot how to relax.

MARCUS
(frustrated)
I can't move my legs. My foot just... drops. And these spasms—

HOUSE
Yeah, yeah. Weakness, spasticity, cold makes it worse. Very dramatic. Also very *wrong* if we're thinking what everyone's thinking.

He taps his cane against the bed frame.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
Blood work's clean. Electrolytes, glucose, muscles aren't dying. So it's not the muscles. Nerve conduction studies came back normal twice. So it's not the wires.

MARCUS
Then what is it?

HOUSE
That's the question, isn't it?

---

**ACT ONE**

INT. DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS ROOM — DAY

The team spreads papers across the table.

CHASE
Cryoglobulinemia. Cold-triggered exacerbation, progressive weakness—it fits.

CAMERON
(shaking her head)
Autoimmune. But systemic, not peripheral. The emotional component matters. Did something happen before this started?

FOREMAN
ALS. We need to rule out motor neuron disease before we chase ghosts.

HOUSE
(sitting, leg extended)
You're all thinking peripheral. Cables, muscles, nerve endings. But what if the problem's upstream? What if his nervous system forgot the most important job it has?

CAMERON
Which is?

HOUSE
Telling things to *stop*.

---

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — DAY

House enters alone. Marcus is rigid, barely able to turn his head.

HOUSE
Your muscles have been staging a mutiny. Congratulations—complete rigidity without any of the fun parts.

MARCUS
What do you mean?

HOUSE
You've stopped dancing at your kid's recital. Stopped reaching for the top shelf. You've basically stopped doing anything that requires your muscles to relax. That's not laziness, Marcus. That's your body forgetting how to let go.

MARCUS
That doesn't make sense.

HOUSE
Sure it does—you're just not listening. What I'm seeing is you've become a human statue who can still walk, which means something's systematically poisoning the very thing that tells your muscles when to chill out. Your nervous system's basically locked in permanent "go" mode.

---

**ACT TWO**

INT. DIAGNOSTIC LAB — DAY

Technicians run EMG tests on Marcus. Needles in his legs. His muscles jump and twitch with each electrical stimulation.

TECH
(into intercom)
Incremental response on repetitive stimulation. Strength actually improves with repeated contraction.

In the observation room, House and Foreman watch the monitors.

FOREMAN
That rules out neuromuscular junction disease.

HOUSE
And ALS. And primary neuropathy. And basically everything we've tested for so far.

FOREMAN
So what's left?

HOUSE
Something that locks the system down from the inside.

---

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM — LATER

House sits beside Marcus's bed. The patient stares at the ceiling, his right calf twitching involuntarily.

HOUSE
ANA panel's negative. No autoimmune markers. Lumbar puncture was clean—no inflammation, no infection. We've eliminated the obvious suspects.

MARCUS
So you don't know what's wrong with me.

HOUSE
Not yet.

He stands, moves to the window.

HOUSE (CONT'D)
But here's what I do know: your muscles aren't dying. Your nerves are conducting signals just fine. Your spinal fluid is clean. You're not having a heart attack. The problem is your brain is forgetting how to send the "off switch" signal to your muscles. It's like someone jammed the brakes, and now everything's stuck in locked-down mode from your spine outward.

MARCUS
My heart?

HOUSE
Your heart's fine—trust me, I'd be way more interested if it wasn't. What I'm talking about is deeper: your brain's losing its ability to send the "off switch" signal to your muscles.

---

**ACT THREE**

INT. DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS ROOM — DAY

House stands at the whiteboard, staring at the test results.

CHASE
We're running out of tests.

CAMERON
There has to be something we're missing.

HOUSE
(tapping marker against board)
What if it's not what we're testing
